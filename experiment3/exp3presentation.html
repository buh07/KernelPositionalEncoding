T1 Deep Dive: SI Head Causal Priority

Theory T1 (simple statement): If a head has high SI score (high shift-invariance R2), then removing it should hurt position-heavy tasks more than removing low-SI heads.
Why this test matters: Exp2 worked at frequency-band level. T1 moves to direct head-level causality by ablating high-SI and low-SI groups.

Experimental Setup

  • Profile per-head R2 on 50 wiki sequences (len=512).
  • Define top and bottom quartile SI head groups.
  • Ablate head outputs (o_proj slices) for high-SI vs low-SI vs random groups.
  • Evaluate on retrieval and local-key tasks across spans.

Results

ModelHigh-SI dropLow-SI dropHigh-Lowp(one-sided)
Llama0.30830.3232-0.01490.999993
OLMo0.08220.0940-0.01180.974726

Conclusion: T1 failed in both models. Removing high-SI heads did not hurt more than removing low-SI heads.

Why: the high-minus-low difference is negative in both models, so the data points in the opposite direction from the hypothesis.

Sources: experiment3/theory1_si_circuits.py (protocol), results/experiment3/theory1_si_circuits/*/analysis.json, experiment3/experiment3results.md

T3 Deep Dive: Cross-Term Coupling vs Pair Importance

Theory T3 (simple statement): The RoPE pairs that matter most should be the pairs where content and position interact most strongly, not just the pairs in a named frequency range.
Why this test matters: It tries to explain Exp2's H2 reversal by looking inside the attention-logit decomposition.

Experimental Setup

  • Compute raw and double-centered diagonal kernels per head.
  • Project gap signal (g_raw - g_centered) onto RoPE pairs.
  • Correlate cross-term magnitude with Exp2 pair ablation effects.
  • Use Pearson, Spearman, and partial correlations.

Method note in protocol: avoid pre-centering raw logits before cross-term analysis.

Llama-0.1101-0.15664
OLMo+0.0496+0.18033

Conclusion: mixed descriptive evidence only. Not strong enough for a yes/no mechanistic claim.

T5 Deep Dive: Continuation-Selectivity Hypothesis

Theory T5 (simple statement): If high-SI heads help continue multi-token words, then ablating them should hurt continuation tokens more than word-start tokens.
Why this test matters: It checks whether SI helps a specific subword function, not just general performance.

Experimental Setup

  • Build continuation vs word-initial token sets (~500 each).
  • Ablate high-SI, low-SI, and random groups.
  • Measure interaction: continuation damage minus initial damage.
  • Run robustness with perplexity-matched subsample.

Results

ModelHigh-Low interaction (reported)Matched robustnessStatus
Llama-1.9623-1.8927Not supported
OLMo-1.4382-1.5760Not supported

Conclusion: T5 failed. The effect goes in the opposite direction from the proposed continuation-selective mechanism.

Why: interaction terms are strongly negative and stay negative in matched-control robustness runs.

Theory T7 (simple statement): SI may sit in earlier "feeder" heads that send positional information to induction heads, rather than in the induction heads themselves.
Why this test matters: It was designed to resolve the tension between induction behavior and low SI scores on known induction heads.

T8 Deep Dive: Positional Kernel Functional Relevance

Theory T8 (simple statement): The SI positional kernel g(Delta) carries real function. If we subtract it from logits, performance should get worse.
Why this test matters: T8 tests causality at the kernel level, not just at head labels.

Experimental Setup

  • Estimate per-head SI kernel from profiling sequences.
  • Subtract the estimated kernel from pre-softmax logits via hook.
  • Compare baseline vs subtract-high-SI vs subtract-low-SI vs subtract-all.
  • Use per-sequence mean loss as statistical unit.

Results

ModelAll-head kernel subtractionHigh-SI subtractionLow-SI subtractionHigh-Low diff
Llama+3.7975+4.1546+0.0736+4.0810
OLMo+0.0710+0.0576+0.0072+0.0504

Conclusion: supported. The positional kernel is load-bearing in both models; subtracting it raises loss.

Magnitude asymmetry note: the all-head effect is much larger in Llama (+3.7975) than OLMo (+0.0710), so the claim is symmetric in sign but not in magnitude. This is a model-conditional strength difference, not equal effect size.

Why: loss increases in the expected direction across subtraction settings, and high-SI subtraction hurts more than low-SI subtraction.

Sources: experiment3/theory8_position_ablation.py, results/experiment3/theory8_position_ablation/*/report.json, experiment3/experiment3results.md

Analysis I: What The Theory Slides Collectively Say

Findings that survived theory-level stress

  • T5b: boundary-linked SI signal remains robust.
  • T8: the positional kernel is clearly load-bearing.
  • T7/T7b: feeder evidence exists, but stronger tests show model dependence.

Findings that were falsified or demoted

  • T1 and T5 simple "high-SI hurts more" stories failed.
  • T10 did not isolate a clean induction-only feeder effect.
  • T3 and T9 are descriptive signals, not confirmatory mechanism closures.

T10 implication: T10's failure directly weakens a strong "induction-only feeder" claim. The safer reading is broader and model-conditional feeder behavior.

T7 vs T7b in Llama: strong T7 correlation can show association without proving feeder causality; T7b's Llama non-support is why this caveat remains necessary.

Narrative shift: SI is real and important, but not as one universal "high-SI dominates everything" circuit story.

Analysis III: Core + Phase2 Integrated Narrative

Integrated story

  1. SI structure exists and has real functional impact (Exp1, T8).
  2. Simple frequency-range mapping is not enough (Exp2 H2 reversal).
  3. Boundary-linked behavior and redundancy are the most stable structural findings (T5b + C.1).
  4. Feeder and specialization effects are real but model- and context-dependent (T7/T7b/J).
  5. Tokenizer strictness outcomes differ across models (B/I multiseed + invariance). This asymmetry is observed, but not yet fully explained.

Working conclusion: SI behaves like shared infrastructure with model-specific implementations, not one universal circuit template.

Experiment 7: Compressed Sensing Theory

A quantitative mathematical framework for shift-invariant structure

Motivation (simple): Experiments 1-6 showed what SI does. Experiment 7 asks why SI appears in the first place. We use compressed sensing (CS) as a math lens: the PE matrix acts like a measurement system, and its geometry should predict SI behavior.

Exp7A: Welch Bound Predicts SI Head R2

Experiment 7A: Compute each model's Welch-bound coherence gap and test whether that number predicts observed SI strength (R2).
Setup: 36 model-config combinations (6 models x 2 seq lengths x 3 data types). Bootstrap with 2000 samples.

Core result

-0.793
Pearson r (coherence gap vs R2)
p < 0.001
Significance
0.665
Fitted alpha parameter

What this means (plain English)

The Welch bound is a limit on how similar PE columns can be. A smaller coherence gap means positions are easier to tell apart. In these runs, models with better PE geometry tend to show stronger SI structure (higher R2).

Correlation: r = −0.793, CI: [−0.891, −0.592]. Spearman rho = −0.510 (p = 0.0015).

Important caveat: we report 36 condition rows, but only 6 independent models (one PE gap per model). So the true independent sample size is n=6, not 36. Read this as suggestive evidence, not a final law.

Conclusion (scoped): 7A is consistent with CS theory and gives a strong pattern (r = −0.793), but because effective n=6 models, this remains theory-consistent evidence, not a confirmed quantitative law.

Why it still matters: even with n=6, the direction is stable, and Exp7B gives a second independent prediction that points the same way. So this is more than a one-off curve fit, but still below "proven" status.

Exp7: Theoretical Framework Summary

The compressed sensing interpretation of SI structure

  1. PE as measurement geometry: positional encodings define the space where heads read position information.
